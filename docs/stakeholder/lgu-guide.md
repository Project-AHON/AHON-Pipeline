# Interpreting AHON evidence

AHON supports discussion of funding, population context and CMCI preparedness components. It cannot yet classify earthquake risk or rank a risk–preparedness gap.

Hazard describes shaking likelihood/severity; exposure describes people/assets in affected locations; vulnerability describes susceptibility to damage; preparedness describes capacity to anticipate/respond/recover. Historical earthquake observations do not establish these together. A locality mentioned in a report is not proof of exposure.

Display reporting year, reference snapshot, source ingestion timestamps and insufficient-data status. Missing means unknown, never zero. Funding utilization can exceed one and requires review; expenditure alone does not establish effective preparedness. Do not add population/funding parent aggregates to their children.

CMCI outputs are selected raw components. Interim pillar sums are not official CMCI scores or a validated preparedness index. Individual resiliency indicators identify subjects for local verification, such as early warning and disaster plans. Missing component evidence indicates a data/review need, not proven poor performance.

Qualified reviewers must approve hazard, exposure, vulnerability, geographic/temporal alignment and any scales, weights or thresholds before classifications or rankings are published. Risk and gap fields stay empty. See [metric definitions](../architecture/metrics.md).
